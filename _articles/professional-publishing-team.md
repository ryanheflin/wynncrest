A professional book is usually the work of more than one specialist.

Even when a single author writes every word, publishing still involves editorial, design, production, metadata, distribution, and project-management work that requires different skills.

## Book strategist or developmental lead

This role helps answer foundational questions:

- What is the book?
- Who is it for?
- How should it be positioned?
- What should the structure accomplish?
- What material is missing?
- What does the project require?

On some projects this is part of [book development](/book-development/). On others, an editor or experienced publishing lead performs the role.

## Author or ghostwriter

The manuscript still has to be created.

The author may draft independently, collaborate with another writer, or work with a professional [ghostwriter](/ghostwriting/).

The writing role is responsible for turning ideas and source material into sustained readable prose.

## Developmental editor

The developmental editor evaluates the manuscript as a whole.

They focus on:

- structure;
- argument;
- pacing;
- logic;
- chapter function;
- reader needs;
- missing material;
- unnecessary repetition.

## Copyeditor

Once the manuscript is structurally stable, the copyeditor works more closely at sentence level.

They may address:

- grammar;
- usage;
- consistency;
- clarity;
- style;
- terminology;
- citations;
- factual presentation.

## Proofreader

A proofreader reviews the near-final typeset book.

This is a different job from editing the manuscript. The proofreader is looking for errors that remain—or were introduced—during the production process.

## Cover designer

The cover must work as both a physical design and a small digital thumbnail.

A professional cover considers genre expectations, positioning, typography, imagery, spine and back-cover requirements, and the technical realities of production.

## Interior designer and typesetter

Interior design determines how the book actually reads on the page.

The designer establishes:

- typography;
- margins;
- page hierarchy;
- chapter openings;
- headings;
- tables;
- figures;
- notes;
- running heads;
- page numbering;
- other repeated elements.

## Production specialist

Production turns approved designs into valid files for print and digital manufacturing.

This work may include:

- print specifications;
- color handling;
- bleed and trim;
- image resolution;
- ebook conversion;
- file validation;
- proof review.

## Publishing and metadata lead

The book must also be correctly represented in publishing systems.

That includes:

- ISBNs;
- title and subtitle;
- contributor names;
- descriptions;
- categories;
- keywords;
- pricing;
- publication dates;
- edition information;
- distribution settings.

## Project manager or publishing lead

Someone has to make sure all of these specialists are working from the right version, in the right sequence, against the right deadlines.

This coordination is one of the least visible and most important parts of a professional publishing process.

Wynncrest's model is built around assembling and managing the team a particular book needs rather than expecting the author to find and coordinate every specialist independently.
